# Wrong Message Sent

An offer, a document request, or a campaign went to the wrong family, the wrong campus list, or went out with the wrong content. This is one of the most likely real incidents in this system, and speed in the first few minutes matters more than anything else you do afterward.

Work through this in order.

## 1. Stop further sends immediately

What you do depends on what kind of message it was:

- **A journey (an automated sequence like a nurture or "Push to Apply" series):** go to Recruitment → Journeys, open the journey, and click **Pause journey**. This stops every future step for every family on that journey network-wide, not just one family. If only one family needs to come off it and the rest is fine, open that family's journey enrollment and use **Remove from journey** instead of pausing the whole thing.
- **A campaign that is still sending (Email Families):** go to the Recruitment page, find it in the "Active + recent campaigns" card, and click **Cancel**. Cancel only stops recipients who have not been sent to yet. Anyone the campaign already reached stays reached; cancelling cannot un-send an email already gone out.
- **A single message you are about to send by hand:** simply do not send it. If you already clicked send, there is no recall; move to step 2.

## 2. Determine exactly who received what

Before you decide anything else, find out the real scope of what went out.

- For a specific family: open their record. A lead's **Journey timeline** on the lead detail page logs calls, notes, and message activity for that family. There is no equivalent message log on the application detail page today; check that application's Internal notes instead, since staff-sent one-off emails to a family get recorded there.
- For a campus-wide view of what was sent, when: Staff → Communications shows a log of messages sent, scoped to the campus you're viewing.
- To see the exact wording of any automated message type (a nudge, a journey step, a campaign template) without guessing: Staff → Communications → Automated Messages shows the real subject and body of every automated message type using sample data, so you can confirm precisely what the family would have seen.

Write down, in plain terms: which families or lists were affected, what the message said, and what channel it went out on (email, text, in-app).

## 3. Decide whether a correction message is needed, and get approval first

**This is not a decision you make alone.** Whether to send a correction, and what it should say, needs sign-off from your system administrator or network leadership before anything goes out. A poorly worded correction can do more damage than the original mistake, especially if it draws more attention to information that should not have gone out in the first place.

Bring your leadership contact: what happened, who was affected, and your draft of what a correction would say (if one is needed). Wait for their go-ahead.

## 4. Send the correction only after approval

Once approved, send the correction through the same channel families received the original message on, so it reaches them the same way the mistake did.

## 5. Write down what happened and when

Log a plain record: what was sent, to whom, when you caught it, when sends were stopped, and what correction (if any) went out and when. Do this even if the mistake feels small. A record now is much easier than reconstructing the timeline later if a family or your authorizer asks about it.

## 6. Escalate to your system administrator

Tell your system administrator about the incident regardless of how it turned out. They may need to check whether the mistake is systemic (a template problem, a list-building mistake) rather than a one-time slip, and they are the ones who can look at what the audit trail actually captured for the action that caused it.

## What counts as a privacy incident

A privacy incident is specifically **a message that exposed one family's information to another family** — for example, a document request or offer that named or attached the wrong student's information, or a campaign list that let one family see another family's name or details.

**Privacy incidents go to leadership the same day, not the next business day.** Student information is protected by law, and a delay in reporting is itself a problem on top of the original mistake. If you are not sure whether what happened counts as a privacy incident, treat it as one and escalate immediately; do not wait to be certain.

## Hardest questions

**I sent an offer to the wrong family but nothing about another family's information was exposed. Is this still urgent?** Yes. Wrong content or a wrong seat offer is a trust and accuracy problem even without a privacy exposure, and it can affect a family's decisions (a deadline they think applies to them, a seat they think they have). Follow steps 1 through 6 regardless of whether it rises to a privacy incident.

**Can I just quietly fix the data and say nothing to the family?** Only after leadership has decided that no correction message is needed. Quietly fixing a record without deciding whether the family needs to know is not your call to make alone; that is exactly what step 3 exists to catch.

**The campaign already fully sent before I noticed. Is there any point in cancelling it?** Cancel it anyway if it is still shown as sending; it stops anyone still pending. If it shows complete, skip straight to step 2 and find out exactly who it reached.

Only your system administrator can look deeper into how a mistake happened (an audit trail entry, a list-building error, a template problem) or determine whether an incident needs to be reported beyond the network.
